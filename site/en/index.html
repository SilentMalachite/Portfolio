A11yLab

Different ways of seeing, hearing, and moving call for different approaches to accessing information. A11yLab is a space for exploring these differences and writing about accessibility.

Articles explore captions, contrast, and keyboard navigation through concrete examples. Writing about live text support for spoken communication also explains the motivation behind my macOS app. The aim is to connect understanding with making.

Articles and the website are created in collaboration with AI, sharing ideas and the thinking behind the projects.

SummaryTalkFrom speech to text. 02 / COMMUNICATION
Communication access / macOSPublic source

Another way to access spoken information.

SummaryTalk

Access to speech in written form helps people follow conversations and receive information. SummaryTalk is a macOS app I am developing for real-time transcription and support for human-assisted live text communication.

It captures both microphone input and system audio, such as audio from meeting apps, and converts speech into text. It also supports integration through IPtalk-compatible communication. Audio capture, recognition, and data exchange are separated into distinct responsibilities within the delivery workflow.

Design and current status
Implemented in Swift / SwiftUI. Parts of the IPtalk protocol support have limitations that still require verification.

ARCHAEOLOGY & WELFARE AlchemIIIFHeritage knowledge. Meaningful work. 03 / CULTURAL HERITAGE
Archaeology × Welfare / Web appPublic source

Preserving heritage through meaningful work.

AlchemIIIF

As an archaeology specialist, I want to put my knowledge of cultural heritage to use in welfare. AlchemIIIF explores that connection. It aims to turn the digitization of archaeological excavation reports into meaningful work that contributes to preserving cultural heritage, within Japan’s continuing employment support services for people with disabilities.

The design gives archaeological researchers a way to publish their materials and gives the people doing the work a more approachable process. It connects PDF-to-image conversion, figure cropping, metadata entry, review and approval, and publication in one workflow. Support for IIIF, a shared framework for publishing and viewing digital materials, brings archaeological and welfare needs together through technology.

Design and current status
Built with Elixir / Phoenix and PostgreSQL. The public README presents the workflow and screenshots; the repository documents the architecture and deployment process.

OmniArchiveFrom documents to shared knowledge. 04 / DIGITAL ARCHIVES
Digital publishing / Web appPublic source

Connect the work of organizing materials to publication.

OmniArchive

OmniArchive is a web app for creating digital archives from PDFs and images, helping turn documents into materials people can find, read, and use. Its approach to document handling and publication is informed by the context of Japan’s continuing employment support services for people with disabilities.

The workflow moves through image cropping, metadata entry, review and approval, and publication. It uses IIIF to publish and view materials in a shared format, aiming for both clarity for the people doing the work and usefulness after publication.

Design and current status
Built with Elixir / Phoenix, with cognitive accessibility as a design principle. The source code is public.

MY APPROACH

Start with people.
Respond with technology.

Keep learning and checking,
even after building.

01

Start with people and real-world constraints.

Before choosing technology, I ask who is facing a difficulty, where, and why. In A11yLab and AlchemIIIF, those questions guide my writing and design.

02

Check the evidence.

I do not treat an AI suggestion or a review finding as a conclusion on its own. As in agent-review-ts, I combine independent perspectives with verification to make decisions.

03

Improve in small steps.

I distinguish what works from what still needs checking. As in SummaryTalk, making limitations visible helps guide the next improvement.
